# ADR-0019: Spaces and bookings (Phase 6a, 6b)
Status: Proposed. Builds on [ADR-0008](0008-facilities-core-model.md) and [ADR-0006](0006-outbox-and-worker.md).

## Decisions
- **Two modules, two schemas** (`Fms.Spaces` / `spaces`, `Fms.Bookings` / `bookings`), each with its own migrations and row-level security on every table. They never reach into Facilities' tables: a space is a *Space*-type location, asked for through `IFacilityLookup`, which gained `LocationsUnderAsync` (a place and everything beneath it).
- **Spaces add meaning to locations, not copies of them.** A profile (kind, capacity, notes) is optional per space; a space without capacity cannot be allocated and is left out of occupancy totals (and the screen says how many are).
- **Floor plans are images with pins** (the customer's decision). Only PNG, JPEG and WebP are accepted (decided from content; never SVG or PDF, which can carry script) up to 10 MB, shown with a `sandbox` content-security header. A new drawing supersedes the old one (kept) and **pins survive it** because a pin is a position as a fraction (0 to 1) of the image. A space pin must be on that floor; an asset pin must be in a place on that floor. Pins can be moved by clicking or by typing a percentage, so the screen works without a mouse.
- **Allocation never exceeds capacity.** An allocation gives a person (one place) or a team (a head count) a space for a period, open-ended or not. A new one is accepted only if the *peak* number of people on any day of its period, with those already there, fits (an event sweep over start dates, unit-tested). The check and the insert run under a per-space lock in one transaction, so eight people asking for the last three places get exactly three (proven by a parallel test). Lowering a capacity below the people already placed is refused. A person cannot hold two overlapping places. Releasing ends an allocation from a day; one that has not started is cancelled; both stay as history.
- **Bookable resources** (rooms, desks, parking, equipment) carry their rules: opening hours and days **in the resource's own time zone**, shortest and longest booking, how far ahead, a buffer after each booking, whether approval and check-in are needed. Times are stored as UTC instants.
- **No double booking, however many ask at once.** Each booking is checked against every held booking (pending, confirmed, checked in), counting the buffer, under a per-resource lock in one transaction. SQL Server has no exclusion constraint, so the lock is the guarantee and a parallel test proves it (eight at once: one wins).
- **Approval is maker-checker.** A resource that needs approval holds the slot as *pending*. Whoever asked can never decide it, whatever their rights (a manager who books the hall cannot approve it). Rejecting needs a reason and frees the slot. Approvers (never the requester) and then the organizer are told through the outbox.
- **Check-in and no-show.** Check-in opens 15 minutes before the start. For a resource that requires it, a booking not checked in within the grace period is released by a worker once a minute, so a room is not blocked all morning by someone who did not come.
- **Weekly series** (up to 26 weeks) are all-or-nothing and keep their local time across clock changes.
- **Calendar feed, one way.** A person makes a secret address that a calendar app can subscribe to; it lists only their own bookings. The address is shown once and only its SHA-256 is kept; a new address stops the old; the public route gives the same answer for every wrong address. Text is escaped and lines folded per RFC 5545 so no title can inject a property.
- **Calendar sync is an interface** (the customer's decision): `ICalendarAdapter` and a fake adapter (in memory, only if `Calendar:AllowFakeAdapter` is true). Changes go through the outbox (`calendar.sync`), so a booking never waits on a calendar and a failure is retried. The event reference is kept **in its own table**, not on the booking, because writing it to the booking would change the booking's row version and make the organizer's next cancel fail as "changed by someone else" (a defect found by a test). Microsoft 365 and Google adapters are for when there is a tenant to test against.

## Consequences
- An allocation is by date, not by hour; hot-desking is bookings, not allocations. The two are not linked: a booked desk can sit in an allocated space.
- No edit of a booking (cancel and rebook), no recurrence other than weekly, no rooms with several sections, no equipment kits, no catering or visitor links on a booking.
- Floor plans are raster images: no scale, no layers, no CAD or BIM.
